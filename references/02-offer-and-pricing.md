# 2. Offer and pricing

## 2.1 Offer anatomy

Every offer must close these six elements. Missing any one = a vague offer.

| Element | Question it answers | Quality criterion |
|---|---|---|
| **Result** | what changes in the client's life? | concrete noun + number when one exists |
| **Timeframe** | how long? | a real deadline, not an aspirational one |
| **Mechanism** | why does your way work? | a proper name for the method (gives ownership) |
| **Proof** | who has already had this result? | named and authorised |
| **Risk** | what happens if it doesn't work? | guarantee with calculated exposure (§2.5) |
| **Price** | how much, in what format? | anchored to the alternative, not to your cost |

### Signs of a vague offer (rewrite before going on)
"complete solution" · "digital transformation" · "strategic partnership" · "increases productivity"
**with no number** · "we serve companies of all sizes" · "tailored for you" without saying what changes.

## 2.2 The 6 pricing models

Always evaluate all six and choose **one** as the main model. Mixed models are allowed, but must be declared.

| Model | When it fits | Risk for you |
|---|---|---|
| **Per project** (fixed scope) | clear scope, low change | scope grows, margin falls |
| **Per hour / day** | undefined scope, consulting | punishes your efficiency; client controls the pace |
| **Monthly subscription** | continuous value, recurring product/service | must prove value every month |
| **Per usage / outcome** | measurable, auditable result | requires reliable measurement and an agreed criterion |
| **Per seat / user** | value scales with adoption | if adoption doesn't happen, the client cancels |
| **Base + success** | client wants to share risk | cash flow and billing become complex |

## 2.3 The 3 tiers — never just one

With **a single option**, the client's decision becomes binary: "buy or don't buy". With three, it becomes "which one do I buy". This reduces indecision (see JOLT in `references/03-sales-methodologies.md`).

| Tier | Role | How to design it |
|---|---|---|
| **Essential** | solves the main pain, no extras | the minimum that delivers the promised result |
| **Recommended** | **the one you want to sell** | mark it as recommended — explicit recommendation reduces indecision |
| **Complete** | upper anchor | makes the recommended tier look reasonable; need not be the best seller |

**Common mistake:** putting the best value-for-money in the cheapest tier. The recommended tier must be both the highest-margin **and** the best result for the client — if those two don't coincide, the design is wrong.

## 2.4 Unit economics — mandatory before presenting

For **each** tier, calculate:

```
Direct delivery cost      = hours × cost/hour + supplies + third parties
Gross margin              = price − direct cost
Margin %                  = gross margin / price
Break-even point          = allocated fixed cost / gross margin per unit
```

**Hard rule:** a tier with negative margin, or with a break-even point above your capacity, **does not go into the proposal**. Without this calculation you are selling in order to lose money.

## 2.5 Guarantee with exposure mathematics

Before promising any guarantee:

```
Exposure = (probability of being triggered) × (cost of honouring it)
```

- If `Exposure > tier margin` → **do not offer that guarantee**, or restrict the trigger.
- Defensible guarantees: redo the work at no cost **within the written scope** · return the difference against an agreed, auditable metric · exit period with notice · professional services included up to the value.
- **Never promise a result that depends on the client** (e.g., "you will make X in revenue").

Frame the guarantee as a **trust deposit** (§1.3), not as a disguised discount.

## 2.6 Framing mechanisms

| Mechanism | How to apply | Caution |
|---|---|---|
| **Anchoring** | show the more expensive alternative (including "do nothing" and the cost of the problem) before your price | the anchor must be real and verifiable |
| **Decoy** | the complete tier exists to steer the choice toward the recommended one | do not belittle the complete tier; it must be defensible |
| **Annual framing** | show the monthly equivalent **and** the annual discount | make explicit what the annual plan includes |
| **Launch vs. scale** | lower entry price with a declared counterpart (e.g., feedback, case study) | a real end date, not an invented one |
| **Grandfathering** | existing clients keep their price when it rises | **say this out loud** — it is a trust deposit |

## 2.7 Price review checklist

- [ ] Does the offer have the six elements of §2.1?
- [ ] Is the chosen model declared and justified?
- [ ] Are there three tiers, with the recommended one marked?
- [ ] Has the unit economics of each tier been calculated?
- [ ] Has the guarantee (if any) passed the exposure mathematics?
- [ ] Is the price anchored to the client's alternative, not to my cost?
- [ ] Is there an **authorised** case study to support the value?
- [ ] If the client says "it's expensive", do I know which of their numbers is missing? (see §4.3 of `references/04-outbound-and-objections.md`)
