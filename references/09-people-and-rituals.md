# 9. People and rituals

## 9.1 The manager's 4 meetings

| Meeting | Goal | Duration | Guiding phrase | Practical rules |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Daily stand-up** | unblock, align priorities, keep the pace | **10–15 min** | if it passes 10 minutes, something is wrong | what I finished / what I will do / what blocks me · focus on blockers · escalate later, do not solve in the meeting |
| **Monthly direction** | **decide**, not discuss | **2–3 h** | "decide, don't debate" | scope/time trade-offs · risks and escalations · strategic alignment · bring **options**, not problems · no surprises: align beforehand |
| **Weekly delivery review** | turn activity into results | **60–90 min** | "use data, not opinions" | milestones on time / at risk · risks, issues and dependencies · do not accept "almost done" · separate problem discussion from the report |
| **Quarterly review** | assess the system, improve, refine strategy | **½ to 1 day** | "be brutally honest, without blame" | what works and what doesn't · process improvement · team health and velocity · focus on **systems**, not people · turn insight into action with an owner |

**Unifying principle:** an effective meeting produces alignment; alignment produces results. If a meeting does not end with a decision or an owner, it should not have happened.

## 9.2 RACI matrix

| Letter | Role | Definition |
|---|---|---|
| **R** | Responsible | who **carries out** the activity to completion |
| **A** | Accountable | who **decides** and approves the result (there must be **one** per activity) |
| **C** | Consulted | who provides information or input before the decision |
| **I** | Informed | who needs to be kept up to date |

> **Watch the convention:** some literature uses **D = Driver** (does the work) and **C = Contributor** (provides input), with no "R". **Always declare which convention you are using** in the matrix itself, to avoid ambiguity.

**How to apply:** list the activities in the columns and the roles in the rows; fill it in; check that **each activity has exactly one A**; check that nobody has accumulated too many rows.
**What it is for:** aligning expectations, avoiding overlapping responsibility, and ensuring every activity has an owner. It is the document that settles "I thought it was you".

## 9.3 The manager's daily list (10 practices)

1. Clarify the **goal** before assigning work.
2. Identify the **risks** before they occur.
3. Avoid poor communication — **silence creates assumptions**.
4. Document decisions **while they are fresh**.
5. Follow up **without micromanaging**.
6. Remove **blockers** before asking for updates.
7. **Protect the team's focus**.
8. Keep stakeholders informed.
9. End meetings with **clear action items**.
10. **Leave the project better than you found it.**

**Axes:** focus (IQ) + empathy (EQ) + influence (SQ).

## 9.4 The 6 pillars of success

1. **Master the basics** — life cycle, initiation, planning, execution, monitoring, closure.
2. **Plan with purpose** — clear objectives and goals; schedule, costs and resources; risks and mitigation strategy.
3. **Lead people, not just tasks** — clear communication, trust, motivation, conflict resolution.
4. **Execute with excellence** — monitor progress, solve problems, keep focus, give feedback.
5. **Communicate as a leader** — adapt the message to the audience, concise updates, active listening, influence.
6. **Close strong and learn** — deliver what was promised, document lessons, celebrate and pursue improvement.

**Competencies cited:** leadership · communication · planning · problem solving · adaptability · influence · decision-making. **Tools:** Planner, Jira, ClickUp, MS Project, Trello, Excel, Power BI. **Certifications:** PMP, Scrum.

## 9.5 Lean maturity — the 5 levels

| Level | State | Typical mindset |
|---|---|---|
| **1 Reactive** | very low maturity; a problem drives the organisation to act | "we must act quickly and produce" |
| **2 Tool-based** | results come from isolated tools | "Lean tools must be used" |
| **3 Systematised** | Lean becomes a management system | "how do we keep improving our process?" |
| **4 Integrated** | Lean in product development | "how can we support our strategy?" |
| **5 Cultural** | continuous improvement in organisational behaviour | "how can we improve even more?" |

**Indicators per level:** from "unplanned work / late projects / firefighting" (level 1) up to "teams encouraged / projects delivered on time / learning applied continuously" (level 5).
**How to progress:** help people make it happen → support leaders → integrate into the development process → monitor and sustain.

## 9.6 Leadership, authority and delegation

- **Leadership is not authority.** Authority is a starting point; leadership is influence. *"Whoever thinks they lead and has no one following is just taking a walk."*
- **Adapt the style to the situation** (situational leadership): the same leader directs, coaches, supports and delegates according to the person's competence **and** commitment for that task.
- **Servant leadership:** focus on serving the team's needs, promote self-management, shield the team from outside interference and **remove blockers**.
- **Team stages (Tuckman):** forming → storming → norming → performing. **Storming is expected** — conflict midway does not mean a bad team.
- **Management × leadership:** management enforces the process, uses positional power, focuses on "how" and "when"; leadership guides, influences and collaborates, focuses on "what" and "why".

## 9.7 What to do when the outcome does not depend only on you

A very common situation in consulting and service delivery: the client has the power to sabotage delivery.

1. **Separate what you control from what you influence and what is external.** Write the three lists.
2. **Turn expectations into a written agreement:** success criteria, who does what, deadlines with declared dependencies.
3. **Name the client's dependencies at the kickoff** — not after it goes wrong.
4. **Record the deviation immediately** (snowball effect, §6.4 of `references/06-project-delivery.md`).
5. **Review the forecast as a range** instead of promising a single date.
6. **If the client repeatedly does not fulfil their part:** renegotiate scope or deadline in writing, before the deadline slips. Silence transfers the blame to you.
