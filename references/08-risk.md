# 8. Risk

## 8.1 Probability × impact matrix

**Score = Probability (1–5) × Impact (1–5) → from 1 to 25**

**Probability:** 1 Rare (<5%) · 2 Unlikely (5–20%) · 3 Possible (21–50%) · 4 Likely (51–80%) · 5 Almost certain (>80%)
**Impact:** 1 Insignificant · 2 Minor · 3 Moderate · 4 Major · 5 Catastrophic

| Band | Score | Action |
|---|---|---|
| 🟩 **Low** | 1–4 | monitor within tolerance; no dedicated action |
| 🟨 **Medium** | 5–9 | plan and monitor according to tolerance |
| 🟧 **High** | 10–19 | assign an owner and plan a priority response |
| 🟥 **Extreme** | 20–25 | require immediate action, including reassessing the scope |

**How to use:** identify (impact and probability) → plot on the heat map → prioritise (considering risk, urgency and governance limits) → act and review.
**Different scopes:** the matrix assesses **risks**; **opportunities** need their own impact-benefit criteria, assessed separately.
**Basis of assessment:** **inherent** (no controls applied) × **residual** (after mitigation).

## 8.2 The 5 strategies — threats

| Strategy | When | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Accept** | low probability and low impact | record and **monitor** — accepting is not ignoring |
| **Avoid** | high probability and high impact | extend the deadline when the supplier always delays; reduce unrealistic scope |
| **Transfer** | high impact, low probability | insurance; a third party who takes on the test |
| **Mitigate** | high probability **or** high impact | reduce the probability (process, testing) or the impact (plan B, reserve) |
| **Escalate** | outside your authority | takes it to leadership, who become responsible |

## 8.3 The 5 strategies — opportunities

| Strategy | When |
|---|---|
| **Accept** (observe) | low probability and low impact — not worth spending resources |
| **Exploit** | high probability and high impact — make it certain (e.g., a volume-purchase discount) |
| **Share** | high impact, low probability — with a third party who helps make it happen |
| **Enhance** | add resources to raise probability or impact |
| **Escalate** | outside your role |

**Point of attention:** a **positive** risk is an opportunity and deserves active treatment. Most teams only look at threats and lose the available gain.

## 8.4 Reserves — the distinction that avoids arguments

| Type | Covers | Who controls it |
|---|---|---|
| **Contingency** | **known-unknown** risks: foreseen, with a planned response | the project manager |
| **Management** | **unknown-unknown** risks: outside the foreseen scope | leadership |

**Examples:** contingency → 15 extra days of schedule if the feasibility test fails · management → a political or social change that affects shipment of goods.

**Reserve analysis:** check whether the remaining reserve is still sufficient for the remaining risk. If you are 25% through the project and 80% of the reserve is consumed, **that is a problem** — and it may require a new baseline with leadership approval.

## 8.5 Making risk management work

- **Review rhythm:** include risk in the **weekly agenda**. This makes the team proactive rather than reactive; a risk left in a drawer only surfaces when it becomes a problem.
- **Register with an owner:** every risk has a named owner, a planned response and a review date (`templates/risk-register.md`).
- **Document the response:** a risk without a written response is a risk accepted by omission.
- **Ask about opportunity:** "what could go **better** than planned, and how do we raise the chance of it?"

## 8.6 Measurement pitfalls (they contaminate risk and performance analysis)

| Pitfall | What it is | How it shows up |
|---|---|---|
| **Confirmation bias** | seeking only data that confirms your view | a report that ignores the bad variance |
| **Correlation ≠ causation** | two variables move together, but one does not cause the other | "budget overruns always cause delays" — not a rule |
| **Unrealistic targets** | an impossible target demotivates instead of motivating | disengaged team, manipulated data |
| **Hawthorne effect** | measuring changes behaviour | the team optimises the metric, not the result |
| **Wrong metric** | measuring what does not matter | a lot of effort, no better decisions |
| **Vanity metric** | looks good in a report, useless for decisions | numbers that impress leadership and change nothing |

**Practical countermeasure:** before choosing a metric, ask *"what decision does this metric change?"*. If none, do not collect it.

## 8.7 Risk review checklist

- [ ] Are the risks recorded with cause, effect and owner?
- [ ] Do probability and impact use the **same scale** for everyone?
- [ ] Have **residual** risks been assessed after the responses?
- [ ] Is at least one **opportunity** being actively treated?
- [ ] Does the contingency reserve still cover the remaining risk?
- [ ] Are the responses realistic, cost-effective and agreed with the parties?
- [ ] Is risk on the weekly agenda and the monthly review?
- [ ] Have risks outside my authority been escalated in writing?
