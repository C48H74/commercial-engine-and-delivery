# 12. Validation, consultative selling and negotiation — the Brazilian school

> **Why this file exists:** the methods in files 03 and 05 were designed in markets with abundant capital and mature processes. This file brings the view of people who operated in Brazil, where **scarce resources are the rule, not the exception** — and where that changes the order of things.

---

## 12.1 Rony Meisler (Grupo Reserva) — validate before structuring

### Testing fast beats planning at length
- **Too much planning delays learning about the business.** Learning comes from contact with the market, not from prior analysis.
- **A perfect product arrives late.** Launching without flaws usually means launching after the market has moved on.
- **Experimenting at small scale and with speed** is what makes it possible to get it right at scale later.
- The cost of error must be **small enough to fit in your pocket**. It is this — not the methodology — that allows fast mistakes.

### The critique of imported frameworks
Foreign methodologies and terminology **often ignore Brazilian reality**: extreme adversity, scarcity of resources, a market without the same access to capital.
**Practical consequence:** the Brazilian entrepreneur is forced to **test immediately**, because **there is no budget** for large upfront investments — and improves the offer progressively.
> In other words: what abroad is a methodological choice ("let's build an MVP"), here is a **cash imposition**. Whoever understands this stops blaming themselves for not having the "ideal process" and starts using the constraint as a method.

### The client is the only authority
- The **client defines the business**; the founder's personal preference is secondary.
- The apparel sector is **dominated by authorial vanity** — designers position themselves as sovereign protagonists of the trend.
- Not coming from that egocentric background, Reserva's partners placed **the buyer at the centre of all decisions** and built management focused on **service and experience above the standard**.

**Anti-pattern this item combats:** attachment to the product. Signs — defending what was made despite feedback; blaming non-purchase on the market's lack of education; "my product is good, the customer doesn't understand".

### How Reserva was born (the method in action)
1. **Spontaneous observation of daily life:** several students at the gym wearing the same shorts (2005).
2. **Reading the gap:** lack of alternatives — or conformity in consumption.
3. **Production without factory structure:** the partners made the first pieces.
4. **Door-to-door sales to close friends** — the inner circle as the first test group.
5. **Reinvesting cash from sales into the next line** (T-shirts, new collections) — organic expansion.

**What this teaches:**
- The hypothesis comes from a **gap visible in everyday life**, not from market research.
- The **close circle is the first test group** for commercial traction.
- **Cash from sales finances expansion** — growth without outside capital.

## 12.2 Alfredo Soares (G4 Educação) — consultative selling

### Professional salesperson × order taker

| Order taker (passive) | Consultative salesperson (professional) |
|---|---|
| serves and reacts to the request | **educates** the client |
| accepts any demand | leads a **careful investigation** |
| presents the product | **makes value tangible** in the offer |
| closes on volume | **refuses** deals outside the ICP |
| sells and disappears | makes sure the client can use it |

**Three rules that define the professional:**
1. **Discipline to refuse.** A rigorous consultative process **forbids** selling to someone outside the ICP. Closing an inadequate deal just for the financial volume should be **penalised** — not rewarded. (Ties to link 5 of the diagnosis: selling to the wrong client generates churn.)
2. **Diagnose before recommending.** The role resembles **preventive diagnosis**: before closing, confirm that the **buyer's infrastructure can support the solution**. The metaphor used is direct: **check the voltage of the socket** before plugging in the appliance.
3. **Selling as safe problem resolution**, not as persuasion. The goal is to avoid post-purchase frustration, which is worse than not selling.

### Segment in the communication, not in the product
- Segmentation happens **first in the channels and the narrative**, and only afterwards (if necessary) in manufacturing.
- **The message is the primary tool** for attracting and selecting the audience.
- **Example cited:** G4's courses are not made exclusively for doctors, but dedicated campaigns address the pains and context of that segment — connecting it to the unified corporate product.
- **Gain:** reaches the niche **without expensive productive customisation**. You keep the scale of the central product and change the entry door.

> **Direct application:** before creating a version of the product for niche X, try a **campaign in niche X's language** for the current product. It is cheaper and tests the demand hypothesis.

### Be useful — the competence of the modern salesperson
Declared origin of the competence: **welcoming, listening and care** (inherited from the mother) combined with **logical reasoning, discipline and pragmatism** (from the father). Translated: **empathy + structure**.
To work with excellence, the professional must:
1. **Generate real value**
2. **Hold attention**
3. **Arouse desire or need**
4. **Orchestrate the solution**

**Central point:** it is **not essential to own the answer** — the value lies in being **the link connecting the pain to the right resource**. The concern is with the **usefulness of the conversation**, not with mechanically extracting money. Whoever delivers usefulness reaps financial results **as a consequence**.

## 12.3 Negotiation preparation

### Predictability defuses conflict (the Disney effect)
- In Disney parks, customers **tolerate long queues in intense heat** because there is **absolute predictability of the waiting time**.
- At the doctor's office, an **unexplained** delay causes immediate irritation.
- **Conclusion:** it is not the waiting that irritates — it is the **uncertainty** of the wait.
- **Basis of a solid negotiation:** establish **in advance** the rules, deadlines and mutual commitments, ensuring convergence between what was promised and what will be delivered.

> **Apply to any commercial promise:** give the deadline **and the path**. "It takes 30 days" irritates less than silence; "there are 3 stages, you will get an update every 10 days" almost eliminates the anxiety. This is the same rule as §1.4 (promise only what you control), seen from the side of the **process**.

### Take control of the negotiation
The classic demonstration: ask "would anyone like water?" in each room → **everyone declines**. Walk in **with the bottles in hand** and offer a closed choice ("sparkling or still?") → **immediate acceptance**.

**What this proves:** the difference is not the product, it is **the architecture of the decision**.
- ❌ Open question: transfers to the client the burden of deciding **whether** they want it.
- ✅ Binary alternative: transfers only the decision of **which**.
- **Making it tangible** (showing it, handing it over) reduces inertia.

> **Link with JOLT (file 03):** action **O — offer a recommendation** says the same thing in other words. JOLT explains **why** it works; the demonstration shows **how** to do it without seeming pushy. Use both together.

### Salesperson × negotiator

| Ordinary salesperson | Negotiator |
|---|---|
| focus on their own goals and commission | focus on **lasting exchange** |
| tries to change the view of the negative points | **reframes** with transparency, without falsifying |
| "winning the argument" | **both leave with legitimate gain** |
| concession as defeat | concession as **currency for exchange** |

The negotiator operates on a broader logic: **balances concessions and aligns interests** so the outcome consolidates **mutual victory**.

> **Terminology note:** this is what the literature calls **integrative negotiation** (win-win, interests instead of positions) — as opposed to **distributive** negotiation (splitting the pie). The Brazilian school reaches the same conclusion through market practice.

## 12.4 How this file changes the others (read this section)

### Where validation enters the funnel
File 05 presents the engine in 12 steps with gates. The Brazilian school inserts a **gate before step 0**: **has the demand hypothesis been tested with real money?**

```
[+ NEW] Validation with real money  →  test at small scale, effective charging
    ↓ (only advance with financial traction)
0. Context and intake  →  1. Ideal client  →  2. Offer  → ...
```

**Why this matters:** structuring offer, price and outbound before knowing whether anyone **pays** is the most expensive mistake at the start of a business. And it is the most common precisely because it "looks professional".

### The real-money test
Validation **only counts if there is payment**. Praise, "I found it interesting", "when you launch I want one" are **rung 1 of the evidence ladder** (file 01): there is conversation, there is no traction.

### Disciplined refusal × funnel gate
Alfredo Soares (refusing sales outside the ICP) and the "Qualified" gate (file 05) are the same rule stated from two places: **one on the professional's side, one on the process's side**. Selling outside the profile compromises link 5 (retention) and burns reputation.

## 12.5 The tension you need to know (do not ignore)

| Imported frameworks | Brazilian school |
|---|---|
| validate with research and a structured MVP | **sell first, adjust later** |
| plan the experiment | **learn in the market**; the plan delays |
| available capital for the initial investment | **no budget**; the constraint is the method |
| methodology as process | **practice as process**; foreign terminology is not useful |
| strategic choice to test | **cash obligation** to test |

> **Review note:** the left-hand column is oversimplified. Testing with an MVP and learning from the market is already the core of *Lean Startup*; the real difference lies in emphasis and available capital, not in principle. Use the table as a working hypothesis, not as a clash of schools.

**How to live with both:** use the frameworks to **structure what already has traction** and the Brazilian logic to **discover what does not yet have it**. Testing fast does **not** dispense with rigour — it trades the rigour of prior analysis for the rigour of **honestly measuring the test's result**.
The error is not testing fast; it is testing fast and **not looking at the result**.
