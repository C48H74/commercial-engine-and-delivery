# 10. Standards and benchmarks

> ⚠️ **Origin warning:** the two standards below were synthesised from **third-party presentations** (a certification webinar and a preparation course), **not from the official text**. Before quoting a clause of a standard in a client document, **consult the published text**.

---

## 10.1 ISO 21502:2020 — project management

### The central change: from processes to practices
ISO 21502 **dropped the process model with inputs and outputs** and moved to a **practices** model. Practical consequence: **nothing in the standard is mandatory** — everything is "should" or a recommendation. The standard provides **guidance**; the *how* is up to the organisation.

> This explains why so many people "broke" ISO 21500 without knowing it: the inputs and outputs were never mandatory, but the format gave that impression. ISO 21502 resolved the ambiguity by removing them.

### Five central elements
1. **Project governance** — structures that give transparency and accountability
2. **Stakeholder engagement** — understanding and meeting needs
3. **Risk and opportunity management**
4. **Planning and execution**
5. **Performance measurement**

### What ISO 21502 adds to ISO 21500
- The project is **integrated with strategy** (portfolio → programme → project) — it **stops being an island**.
- Focus on **outcomes, organisational change and benefits**, not just deliverables: the project delivers → generates improvement → realises a benefit.
- Projects can arise from **threats**, not only from opportunities.
- **Integrated practices** organised around the life cycle: **pre-project** (concept, feasibility, business case), direction, control, delivery and **post-project** (transfer, improvements, benefits).
- **Individual practices**, with two new ones: **benefits management** and **organisational change**.
- **Formalised roles:** sponsor · project manager · work package leader · **project assurance** · project office (where one exists).
- **At least two roles cannot be the same person:** the **business/sponsor** side and the **manager/team** side — because of conflict of interest.
- **Project assurance** acts as the sponsor's "eyes and ears" without exercising oversight: it checks whether the project follows the agreed objectives.
- **Shared responsibility:** success or failure **is not the manager's alone**.
- Recommends **assessing the organisation itself** before adopting project management, with **continual improvement** — a decision for leadership, not for the manager.

### The 5 biggest errors, according to ISO 21502
1. Unclear objectives
2. Ineffective communication
3. Weak stakeholder engagement
4. Inadequate risk management
5. Not reviewing progress throughout the cycle

### On agility
The standard is **neither agile nor predictive**: it is a shell that can be used lightly or heavily. The structure may seem heavy for agile methods, but since everything is conditional, it can be applied very leanly — agility depends on the **implementation**, not on the text of the standard.

### The individual practices (what to do in each area)
Scope (define, validate, control) · schedule (estimate durations, prepare the schedule) · resources · costs · risks · quality · **benefits** · **information and reporting** · **organisational change** · planning · lessons learned · change control.
They apply **at any point** in the life cycle, as needed.

## 10.2 ISO 31000:2018 — risk management

### What it is
**A guidance standard, not a certification standard.** Practical rule: standards ending in **"01"** (9001, 27001, 22301) implement PDCA and **certify**; 31000 **guides**. A short document (~16 pages; ABNT publishes it as **ABNT NBR ISO 31000**).

### Structure
`1 Scope · 2 Normative references (none) · 3 Terms and definitions · 4 Principles · 5 Framework · 6 Process`

### Concept
**Risk** = effect of uncertainty on objectives — it can be **negative** (a threat) **or** an **opportunity**.
The two verbs that sum it up: **create value** (seize an opportunity) and **protect value** (avert a threat).
Key defined terms: risk · risk management · stakeholder · risk source · event · consequence · likelihood · control. (More terms in ISO Guide 73.)

### The 8 principles
1. **Integrated** — embedded in activities, not separate
2. **Structured and comprehensive** — consistent and comparable results
3. **Customised** — adjusted to the organisation's context and objectives
4. **Inclusive** — stakeholders' knowledge and perceptions are considered
5. **Dynamic** — risks appear, change and disappear as the context changes
6. **Based on the best available information** — historical and current, with stated limitations and uncertainties
7. **Considers human and cultural factors** — behaviour and culture significantly influence outcomes
8. **Continual improvement** — learning and experience

### The process (clauses 6.2 to 6.7 of the standard — simplified view below)
```
1. Establish the context    → customise, delimit, define criteria
2. Identify                 → find, recognise and describe the risks
3. Analyse                  → understand the nature and measure (quantitatively or qualitatively)
4. Evaluate                 → compare with the criteria and DECIDE whether to treat
5. Treat                    → select and implement the options
   ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
   Continuous support:  communication and consultation · monitoring and review ·
                        recording and reporting
```
> Official clauses, **to be checked against the published text**: 6.2 communication and consultation · 6.3 scope, context and criteria · 6.4 risk assessment (identify, analyse, evaluate) · 6.5 risk treatment · 6.6 monitoring and review · 6.7 recording and reporting. The numbered list above is a simplification, not the structure of the standard.

**Distinction that avoids confusion:** **evaluating** is deciding whether to treat; **treating** is carrying out the treatment.
**Recording and reporting** (a 2018 addition) documents **not only the result but how it was obtained**.

### Framework (clause 5)
Purpose: support the integration of risk management into governance and activities. Cycle: **integration → design → implementation → evaluation → improvement**, with **leadership and commitment at the centre**. Top management support is a condition, not a detail.

### Alternatives mentioned
**COSO ERM** (financial and compliance) · sector standards (automotive, food, aerospace) · all assume at least the **PDCA** cycle: plan → execute/analyse → verify effectiveness → learn.

## 10.3 PMI Pulse of the Profession — figures, **with their status**

> **Status of verification (review of 2026-10-14):** only what is marked **[confirmed]** was checked against the official PMI page. What is marked **[not verified]** must not be quoted as fact until it is checked against the full report. The original infographic figures were **not** validated by this review.

### Pulse 2023 — *Power Skills: Redefining Project Success* [not verified in this review]
- **Sample:** ~**3,500** project professionals.
- **The 4 most critical behavioural competencies** for achieving organisational goals:
  1. **Communication**
  2. **Problem solving**
  3. **Collaborative leadership**
  4. **Strategic thinking**
- Organisations that prioritise these competencies stand out in **benefits realisation maturity**, **organisational agility** and **project management maturity**.

### Pulse 2024 — *The Future of Project Work* (edition and subtitle not confirmed)

**Confirmed on the official PMI page (*Pulse of the Profession® 2024*):**
- **[confirmed]** A **57%** increase in the use of hybrid approaches. The page does not give the base year.
- **[confirmed]** An average project performance rate of **73.8%**, without a detailed definition of the metric.
- **[confirmed]** Teams perform equally well with predictive, hybrid and agile approaches. **The page gives no number of organisations, sample or statistical test.**

**Not confirmed (do not quote until the full report is checked):**
- **[not verified]** Percentages by year: predictive 58% (2020), hybrid 31.5% and agile 24.6% (2023), agile 27% (2022). There is also no basis for "predictive fell 24.3 points", because the table does not give a 2023 value.
- **[not verified]** A study of **477 companies** and a significance test.
- **[not verified]** The **8 high-performance facilitators** with percentages of provision and impact, and the table by industry.
- **[not verified]** The "15th edition" and the subtitle "Moving Past Office-Centric Models".

Conclusion that the page supports: the choice of approach, by itself, does not decide success. Which factors *do* decide it requires the report.

### Other data cited (context, with declared source)
- [not verified in this review] A B2B buyer has already gone through **more than half** of the purchase process before contacting the seller (Gartner).
- [not verified in this review] According to the authors of *The Challenger Sale*, **more than 40%** of consistently high-performing salespeople fit the **Challenger** profile (CEB/Gartner).
- SPIN: the managers' coding was calibrated against standard recordings with a correlation of **0.85**. This is a criterion for coding agreement, **not** a correlation with sales results. The "17% more sales volume among the first 1,000 trained" is **not a claim by Rackham**: it appears as a paraphrase of sceptics' objections in an interview. **Do not use.**
- JOLT (according to the author, **not verified in this review**): about **2.5 million** conversations analysed by machine learning; **~87%** of calls with moderate/high indecision; **~60%** of no-decision losses attributed to indecision.

> **Final rule of use:** third-party figures enter with **report + year**. Figures from the authors of a methodology enter as **"according to the author"**, never as a measured fact. Figures you cannot locate in the source **do not enter**.
