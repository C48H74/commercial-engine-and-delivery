# 4. Outbound and objections

## 4.1 The 5-touch sequence

| Touch | Day | Angle | Goal |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1 | **specific observation** about their business + short question | earn the reply |
| 2 | 3 | **trigger**: news, hire, post, role change, funding round | show you are paying attention |
| 3 | 7 | **common connection** or referral | transfer trust |
| 4 | 14 | **3 concrete ideas** for a challenge they have stated | give value without asking for anything |
| 5 | 21 | **honest close**: *"should I close the file?"* | unlock politely |

### Email rules
- **3 sentences.** If you need more, the problem is the offer, not the email.
- **Soft CTA** in the early touches: a question that can be answered in one line. Do not ask for a meeting.
- Zero flattery, zero jargon, zero "I hope you're well".
- One idea per email. One request per email.

### Honest phrasing (mandatory)
Phrases to **avoid**: "I noticed your company is growing" (generic), "I see you work with X" (says nothing), "we can add value" (empty).
**If there is no real, verifiable observation, do not send the touch.** A generic touch burns the contact for the next four.

### Structure of the first email
```
[Specific, verifiable observation about the business/person]
[How this usually creates problem Y — in one sentence, without exaggerating]
[Short question that can be answered in one line]
```

## 4.2 Anatomy of an objection response

Every objection gets the same structure. Do not respond off the cuff.

```
OBJECTION:    [in the client's words]
WHAT IT IS:   [price | risk | timing | authority | inertia | indecision]
DIAGNOSIS:    [what is really behind it]
RESPONSE:     [acknowledge → return with a question → evidence → next step]
EVIDENCE:     [name, number or document — without this, do not use it]
TRAP:         [what NOT to say]
```

**Canonical order of the response:** acknowledge (without agreeing) → return with a question → only then evidence → propose the next step. **Never** start with the evidence: it sounds like a script.

## 4.3 The two most misdiagnosed objections

### "It's expensive"
**Almost never about price.** It is one of two things:
1. **Unquantified value** — you never put a number on the scale.
   *Action:* return to the pain and quantify it with **their** number (hours × cost/hour × frequency).
2. **Fear of getting it wrong** — the price is the rational justification for an emotional fear.
   *Action:* apply JOLT, item **T** (`references/03-sales-methodologies.md` §3.4).

**Order of response:**
1. **Do not defend the price.** An absolute number without comparison always looks expensive.
2. Quantify the pain with their data.
3. Bring proof from a similar client — **with authorisation**.
4. If they still hesitate, offer **a way out, not a discount**. A discount teaches that your price was fake.
5. Review the tiers: maybe you offered the complete tier to someone who needs the essential one.

**Trap:** "I can get you a discount." It destroys the anchor and does not fix the cause. On the next purchase, the client will ask for a discount again.

### "I'll think about it"
**Not an objection — it is indecision.** Increasing pressure here **makes it worse** (see JOLT). The correct move is to reduce the fear of error: judge the indecision, recommend explicitly, limit the exploration and take the risk off the table.

**Signs that it is indecision, not lack of interest:** the client disappears and comes back, asks for more material, books and cancels calls, praises a lot and decides nothing.

## 4.4 Other common objections and the correct diagnosis

| Objection | Likely diagnosis | First action |
|---|---|---|
| "We already have a supplier" | switching cost + inertia | ask what would make them reconsider |
| "It's not the right time" | real timing or low priority | ask what changes next quarter |
| "I need to talk to my boss" | you haven't spoken to the decision-maker | ask to join the conversation |
| "Just send a proposal" | you skipped discovery | do not send before you have pain + number |
| "Too complex to implement" | execution risk | 30/60/90 plan + services included |
| "We have no budget" | unquantified value **or** a real budget cycle | distinguish the two before acting |
| "My team won't use it" | adoption risk | involve the key user and the champion |

## 4.5 Checklist before sending any outbound

- [ ] Is the touch-1 observation **verifiable** by anyone?
- [ ] Does the email have 3 sentences and **one** request?
- [ ] Is the phrasing honest (no invented urgency, no false scarcity)?
- [ ] Do I know which pain I am assuming — and is it in the profile?
- [ ] Do I have authorised proof ready to use if they reply?
- [ ] Does touch 5 close the file politely (no emotional blackmail)?
