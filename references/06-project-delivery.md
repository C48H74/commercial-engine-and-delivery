# 6. Project delivery — PMBOK 7

> ⚠️ **Mandatory correction:** the 7th edition **dropped the 10 knowledge areas and the 5 process groups** — those belong to the 6th edition. If a source cites "the 10 PMBOK areas", it is out of date. The current structure is **12 principles + 8 performance domains**.

## 6.1 The 12 principles

| # | Principle | What it means in practice |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Stewardship** | act as a responsible adult: integrity, care, reliability, compliance; the project before personal interest |
| 2 | **Collaboration** | a collaborative team environment; individuals and interactions over processes and tools |
| 3 | **Stakeholders** | engage proactively, to the degree needed |
| 4 | **Value** | *value is the final indicator of success*; delivering something worthless on time is not useful |
| 5 | **Systems thinking** | the project is a system within a larger system |
| 6 | **Leadership** | leadership **is not** authority; adapt the style to the situation |
| 7 | **Tailoring** | customise to context; every project is unique |
| 8 | **Quality** | build quality into the process and the deliverables |
| 9 | **Complexity** | navigate it, do not eliminate it |
| 10 | **Risk** | optimise responses, including to **opportunities** |
| 11 | **Adaptability and resilience** | absorb impact and recover quickly |
| 12 | **Change** | enable change to the future state |

## 6.2 The 8 performance domains

Stakeholders · Team · Development approach and life cycle · Planning · Project work · Delivery · Measurement · Uncertainty.

## 6.3 Choosing the approach — predictive, adaptive or hybrid

**Decide by product, team and culture — not by fashion.** (See §10.3: PMI data shows the approach is **not** the determining factor for performance.)

| Factor | Leans **predictive** | Leans **adaptive** |
|---|---|---|
| Risk | high, requires early planning | mitigable through iteration and feedback |
| Regulation / safety | requires phase-by-phase approval | lightly regulated |
| Scope change | expensive or impossible | cheap and frequent |
| Requirements | stable | evolving |
| Innovation | low, known path | high, exploration |
| Delivery cadence | single delivery at the end | incremental deliveries |
| Team | large, distributed, many layers | small (≈7±2), autonomous |
| Culture | many approvals, hierarchy | self-management, decisions taken by the team |

**Hybrid** fits when parts of the product have different natures (e.g., predictive hardware + adaptive software). **The choice must be declared and justified** — not left implicit.

## 6.4 Scope control — the snowball effect

```
1. Small request  →  2. Unrecorded work  →  3. Hidden extra effort
   →  4. Schedule pressure  →  5. Cost increase  →  6. Quality trade-offs
   →  7. Drift from the baseline
```

**How to prevent it, in this order:**
1. **Record the request** — date, requester, description, in a central register.
2. **Assess the impact** on scope, time, cost, quality and risk.
3. **Seek formal approval** before executing.
4. **Update the baselines** (scope, schedule, cost) and the plans.
5. **Communicate** the change to all parties, while the process is still under way.

**Hard rule:** without formal approval, the request **does not go in**. Friendliness without process is what destroys margin. A seemingly small request can derail the project — it is literally the first stage of the snowball effect.

## 6.5 Documents that matter

| Document | Resolves | Closes with |
|---|---|---|
| **Project charter (TAP)** | formally authorises; defines purpose, goals, scope, limits and authority | sponsor's signature |
| **WBS / EAP** | breaks deliverables into work packages and activities | WBS dictionary |
| **Schedule with milestones** | deadlines, dependencies, critical path | schedule baseline |
| **Risk register** | visible exposure, with an owner | planned response per risk |
| **RACI matrix** | who does, who approves, who is informed | agreed with the team |
| **Communication plan** | who needs to know what, when and how | agreed with the parties |
| **Status report** | a decision, not a data dump | actions and decisions needed |
| **Lessons-learned register** | not repeating mistakes | dated entry |

### Project charter — what it includes
Business justification · objectives and success criteria · key stakeholders · scope (what is **inside and outside**) · reference budget and schedule · main risks and assumptions · manager's authority · sponsor approval.

**7 common errors:** starting without a charter · no objectives · poorly defined scope · parties not identified · no milestones · confusing the charter with the project plan · assumptions not documented.
**Flow:** justification → objectives → scope → stakeholders → budget/schedule → risks → sponsor approval → start.

## 6.6 Status report — the 5 blocks

| Block | Content |
|---|---|
| **1. Executive summary** | overall status (green/amber/red) · highlights · achievements · issues · **decisions needed** |
| **2. Project overview** | name and ID · objectives and expected results · scope · sponsor and manager · main parties · period |
| **3. Status** | schedule (planned vs. actual) · costs (budgeted vs. actual vs. forecast) · scope (approved vs. delivered vs. changes) · quality · resources · risks (exposure vs. status) |
| **4. Main details** | period achievements · in progress · next period's priorities · open issues · updated risks · change request status |
| **5. Supporting information** | trends · changes · dependencies · risk register · lessons · attachments |

**Golden rule:** focus on **decisions**, not raw data. Say what you need the reader to decide. A report that asks for nothing is a report nobody reads.
**Good practice:** always use the same format · update regularly · analyse **trends** and variances · highlight what needs action.

## 6.7 Kickoff

**Preparation:** objectives defined · agenda ready · key parties invited · materials ready · tone decided (formal, informal, collaborative).
**Agenda with timings (75 min):** welcome 10 · project overview 15 · scope and objectives 15 · roles and responsibilities 10 · schedule and milestones 10 · communication plan 10 · risks 10 · questions 15 · next steps 10.
**Tips:** arrive early · encourage participation · be clear and concise · promote collaboration · **follow up afterwards** · keep to the agenda · record actions.

## 6.8 The manager's decision tree

**Key principles:** goal-oriented · with sufficient data · involving the right people · considering short, medium and long term · **own the decision** (decide, communicate and take a position).
**Flow:**
```
What decision do I need to make?
  → is it aligned with the objectives and goals?   (no → realign first)
  → do I have enough data and information?          (no → collect, consult experts)
  → does it affect cost, scope, time or quality?    (yes → assess trade-offs and prioritise)
  → who will be affected?                           (consult and get input)
  → what are the risks of each option?              (choose the option with acceptable risk)
  → is there a clearer and better option?
        yes → DECIDE AND ACT → monitor and review
        no  → CREATE OPTIONS (alternatives, reassess, test assumptions) → monitor and review
```

## 6.9 Elements of the project plan (mapped to the domains)

Not a mandatory sequence. The 7th edition of the PMBOK organises work into performance domains (§6.2), not knowledge areas. The elements below are the typical content of a plan, with the domain each one answers to:

| # | Plan element | Performance domain (§6.2) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Define the scope (goals, deliverables, limits) | Planning |
| 2 | Identify the stakeholders | Stakeholders |
| 3 | Set objectives and deliverables | Planning · Delivery |
| 4 | Break down the work (WBS) | Planning |
| 5 | Create the schedule (milestones, dependencies, critical path) | Planning |
| 6 | Plan communication | Stakeholders |
| 7 | Define monitoring and control | Measurement |
| 8 | Manage risks and uncertainties | Uncertainty |
| 9 | Develop the budget | Planning · Measurement |
| 10 | Assign resources and roles | Team |

Items 1 to 10 correspond to what the 6th edition called knowledge areas; here they serve only as a content checklist.

## 6.10 The 8 sprint-planning errors (agile context)

1. Planning without clarity of the goal · 2. Treating an estimate as a fixed deadline · 3. Forgetting the team's **real capacity** · 4. Planning work that is not ready yet · 5. Taking on too much · 6. Ignoring dependencies · 7. No clear sprint goal · 8. Overconfidence.

## 6.11 Good plan vs. excellent plan

| Dimension | Good | Excellent |
|---|---|---|
| **Focus** | complete and deliver | deliver value and realise benefits |
| **Alignment** | linked to needs | linked to strategy and priorities |
| **Scope** | defined and documented | defined, managed, with change governance |
| **Schedule/cost** | basic estimates | baseline, milestones, critical path, control |
| **Risks** | identified and mitigated | managed with a focus on opportunities and resilience |
| **Parties/communication** | aligned when needed | active and transparent engagement |
| **Governance/documentation** | basic structure | traceable, compliant, with lessons |
| **Benefits** | listed | realised and monitored with post-delivery KPIs |
