# 5. Commercial engine — 12 steps, gates and diagnosis

## 5.1 The 12 steps

Each step exists to **close with an artefact**. If the artefact does not exist, the step is not finished.

| # | Step | Module | **Closes with** |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | Context and intake | intake | completed intake (profile) |
| 1 | Ideal client, pain, research | discovery | research notes + ICP definition |
| 2 | Offer | offer | offer statement (`templates/proposal-and-guarantee.md`) |
| 3 | Economics and price | pricing | tier spreadsheet + unit economics |
| 4 | Qualification and discovery | MEDDPICC + SPIN | MEDDPICC scorecard + call guide |
| 5 | Narrative and proof | narrative | written narrative + authorised proof |
| 6 | Risk reduction and scarcity | guarantees | guarantee with exposure mathematics |
| 7 | Pipeline and outbound | outbound | active 5-touch sequence |
| 8 | Objections, indecision, negotiation | objections + JOLT | one-page business case |
| 9 | Buying committee | multithreading | committee map with coverage |
| 10 | Contract and handoff | delivery | contract checklist + handoff document |
| 11 | Delivery, retention, referral, case study | delivery | 30/60/90 plan + case-study consent |
| 12 | Content and AI-driven leverage | content | content plan + AI workflow |

**Order matters:** step 3 depends on step 2; step 4 on steps 1 and 2. Skipping intake (0) contaminates everything.

## 5.2 Funnel stage gates

Do not advance without meeting the criterion. The gate is what stops the pipeline from becoming fiction.

| Stage | Exit criterion |
|---|---|
| **Lead** | fits **2 of 3**: segment, role, trigger |
| **Qualified** | pain **in their words** + **one number** + **named process** |
| **Discovery done** | no "no" on **Pain**, **Champion** or **Process** |
| **Proposal** | business case **accepted** + economic buyer **reached** |
| **Negotiation** | criteria met + **paper process mapped with dates** |
| **Closed** | signed + kickoff + **handoff document** |

**How to use in a pipeline review:** for each deal, ask which stage is declared and which criterion is **not** met. The difference between the two is the next step — or the downgrade.

## 5.3 Bottleneck diagnosis — 5 checks, in order

**Stop at the first broken link.** Do not move on to the next. The order exists because each link limits the ones after it: converting better does not help if few people arrive; selling more does not help if delivery cannot cope.

| # | Check | Diagnostic question | If it fails, the problem is | Action |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Reach** | are enough people seeing the offer? | volume and channel — **not the offer** | more touches, channels, referrals |
| 2 | **Lead quality** | do arrivals have the right profile and trigger? | target and entry message | redefine ICP, trigger and hook |
| 3 | **Conversion** | are the right-profile people buying? | offer, price, proof, objection, indecision | `references/02-*` and `references/03-*` |
| 4 | **Capacity** | can you deliver if you sell twice as much? | operations | **do not sell more until this is fixed** |
| 5 | **Retention** | do buyers stay and refer? | delivery and perceived value | fix before scaling acquisition |

**Classic errors this diagnosis prevents:**
- changing the price when the problem is reach (link 1)
- changing the offer when the problem is the target (link 2)
- scaling acquisition with saturated delivery (link 4) — destroys reputation and generates refunds
- chasing new clients when the problem is that nobody stays (link 5)

**Every output of diagnosis mode ends with exactly ONE experiment card** (`templates/experiment-card.md`), with a mandatory stop rule.

## 5.4 Metrics by link

| Link | Main metric | Quality metric |
|---|---|---|
| Reach | new contacts per week | response rate |
| Quality | % of leads meeting 2 of 3 | qualification rate |
| Conversion | % of qualified deals that close | average sales cycle |
| Capacity | team utilisation | actual vs. promised delivery time |
| Retention | renewal rate | referrals per client |

**Honesty rule:** measure the link where you **can** act. Vanity metrics (followers, impressions, unqualified "leads") do not belong in a commercial-engine report.

## 5.5 30-day sequence (for a full build)

| Day | Action | Steps used |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Qualify the target list | 0, 1 |
| 3 | Build the offer and price tiers | 2, 3 |
| 7 | Outbound to qualified targets | 7 |
| 14 | Prepare discovery and run the first calls | 4 |
| 21 | Map the committee and handle objections/indecision | 8, 9 |
| 30 | Proposal, guarantee and handoff | 10, 11 |

**Do not start all 12 steps at once.** Choose the three that attack the biggest friction and run them.

## 5.6 When to stop investing in a move

Signs that it is time to step back and reformulate, not to push harder:
- link 1 does not close with reasonable volume **and** two test cycles have already passed
- link 2 does not improve after redefining target and message
- acquisition cost is above the margin of the recommended tier (§2.4)
- link 5 shows that the client does not renew and does not refer

In these cases, the correct move is to **declare the verdict "discard"** and reformulate the offer — not to increase effort on the same design.
