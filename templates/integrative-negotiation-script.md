# Script — preparing and running an integrative negotiation

> **Principle:** the salesperson tries to win the argument; the **negotiator** builds an exchange in which both sides leave with legitimate gain. And **takes control** instead of transferring the burden of the decision.

## Objective

Strategically run the negotiation meeting, managing expectations, deadlines and mutually beneficial concessions.

## Required inputs

| Input | Description |
|---|---|
| **Map of both parties' interests** | **essential** objectives × **flexible** points (yours and theirs) |
| **Negotiation limits** | price floor · time ceiling · non-negotiable scope |
| **Predictable schedule** | deliveries and stages, with contact milestones |
| **List of available concessions** | what you can give — and what you expect in return for each one |

> **Without written limits, you do not negotiate: you react.** Define the floor before you walk into the room.

---

## Operational steps

### 1. Align expectations in advance (Disney effect)
State **in advance**: the meeting agenda, the estimated time and the next stages.
Absolute predictability reduces anxiety and increases compliance.
- ❌ "I'll check with the team and get back to you."
- ✅ "There are three stages: proposal in 3 days, review in one week, signature by the 30th. At each stage you'll get an update from me."

### 2. Proactively anchor options
Replace **unstructured open questions** with **closed alternatives with controlled options**.
- ❌ "Do you want to hire us?" → the client decides **whether**
- ✅ "Sparkling or still?" → the client decides **which**
- ✅ "Option A (scope X, timeline Y) or Option B (scope Z, timeline W)?"
- **Make it tangible:** show the material, the document, the screen. Seeing it reduces inertia.

### 3. Map objections and reframe
Neutralise constraints through **transparent and logical contextualisation** — **never falsifying data**.
- Rewrite the negative point in the right context (e.g., higher price = less rework later).
- If the data contradicts you, **say so** — losing the argument while keeping trust is worth it.
- Use the objection library (`references/04-outbound-and-objections.md`).

### 4. Balanced trade of concessions
**For each concession requested, demand a proportional counterpart.** A concession is not a gift.

| If they ask for | Ask for in return |
|---|---|
| discount | upfront payment · larger volume · shorter payment term |
| longer timeline | reduced scope · fewer revisions · later start |
| larger scope | adjusted price · extended timeline · their team allocated |
| exclusivity | longer contract · minimum volume guarantee |
| special condition | authorised case study · referral · payment upfront |

> **Rule:** never give two concessions in a row without a counterpart. The second free concession teaches that the price was negotiable from the start.

### 5. Close with a sense of mutual victory
Recap the agreements **recording the gains on both sides**:
> "So we agree: you get [X] and [Y], I get [Z] and [W]. Is that right?"

Saying the other side's gain out loud consolidates the perception of a fair exchange — and reduces post-signature regret.

---

## Outputs

- **Agreement terms or draft** with commitments, dates and detailed mutual counterparts.
- **Implementation schedule** with regular contact milestones.

---

## Decision criteria

| Verdict | Criterion |
|---|---|
| **Close the agreement** | both sides met their priority objectives **within the safety margins** |
| **Suspend for review** | one party demands **one-sided concessions** without a counterpart, compromising the win-win balance |
| **Refuse the deal** | the terms **exceed the limits of profitability or delivery capacity** |

**Suspending is not losing:** it is the only way to keep bargaining power without leaving the table.

---

## Pitfalls

| Pitfall | Effect |
|---|---|
| Giving in to "not lose the deal" | destroys margin and sets a precedent for the next purchase |
| Asking open-endedly ("shall we close?") | transfers the burden of the decision and increases inertia |
| Surprising the client with a new condition in the meeting | breaks the trust built |
| Lying or hiding negative data | discovered later, costs the client and the referral |
| Negotiating without a defined floor | you accept what you should not and find out in delivery |
| Treating a concession as a gift | teaches that there is always more to give |

## Conceptual distinction (to avoid confusing it with haggling)

- **Distributive negotiation:** dividing a fixed pie — one wins, the other loses.
- **Integrative negotiation:** enlarging the pie and aligning **interests** rather than **positions** — this is what this script conducts. The focus moves away from price and toward the package (timeline, scope, risk, guarantee).
