# Script — idea validation and quick market test

> **Principle:** validation **only counts if there is payment**. Verbal praise is rung 1 of the evidence ladder — there is conversation, there is no traction. Charging is the experiment.

## Objective

Test commercial value hypotheses with the **smallest possible financial outlay**, before structuring a full operation (factory, team, system, inventory).

## Required inputs

| Input | Description | If missing |
|---|---|---|
| **Core hypothesis** | the problem and for whom | do not start — it is the question of the test |
| **Initial audience** | primary circle or accessible community with compatible purchasing power | use whoever you reach **today** |
| **Minimum solution** | low-batch prototype or landing page | no structure before selling |
| **Test budget** | restricted and declared amount | without a limit, the test becomes half a business |
| **Maximum duration** | **7 to 15 days** | a long deadline kills the learning |

---

## Operational steps

### 1. Formulate the MVP
Produce the **smallest viable batch**: a few units, or a simulated demonstration, or a landing page. The goal is **not** final quality — it is to enable the transaction.

### 2. Test in a restricted environment
Approach **primary-circle clients** or an easily reached community, with compatible purchasing power. No intermediaries, no paid advertising: direct contact.

### 3. Collect transactions directly
Make the real offer **with immediate monetary charge**.
Record: who bought, how much, when, and who **refused and why**.
> The refusal is as valuable as the purchase. Note the objection word for word.

### 4. Collect operational feedback
Ask the **buyers** (not the curious) about:
- usability and functionality in real use
- what disappointed them
- price perception (expensive, fair, cheap)

### 5. Quick portfolio adjustment
Identify bottlenecks and redefine attributes **before** any investment in industrial scale or hiring a team.

---

## Outputs

- **Preliminary financial traction report:** conversion rate and revenue from the pilot batch.
- **Prioritised list of critical improvements, ranked by clients** (not by you).
- **Reinvestment plan** for the initial revenue into a second experimental cycle.

---

## Decision criteria

| Verdict | Criterion |
|---|---|
| **Advance to scale** | **more than 60% of the batch sold**, with spontaneous repurchase **or** organic referrals |
| **Pivot / adjust communication** | manifest interest, but hesitation at payment → adjust offer, narrative or price |
| **Close / discard the hypothesis** | no financial traction **even after adjusting the approach** in two distinct groups |

**Honesty rule:** do not change the success criterion after seeing the result. If you decided 60%, it is 60% — even if it hurts.

---

## Pitfalls that invalidate the test

| Pitfall | Why it invalidates it |
|---|---|
| Validating by praise | "I loved it" is not traction (§1.2 of the evidence reference) |
| Selling only to friends who buy out of kindness | measures friendship, not demand — separate who would buy from a stranger |
| Batch too large | destroys the logic of low cost of error |
| Long deadline | the market changes and the learning loses value |
| Not noting the refusals | the objection is the most valuable input for the next cycle |
| Structuring before validating | the most expensive mistake at the start — and the most common |
