## Legend used in this matrix

| Letter | Role | Definition |
|---|---|---|
| **R** | **Responsible** | who carries out the activity to completion |
| **A** | **Accountable** | who decides and approves the result — **one only per activity** |
| **C** | **Consulted** | who provides information before the decision |
| **I** | **Informed** | who needs to be kept up to date |

> ⚠️ **Declare the convention.** There is a variant that uses **D = Driver** (does the work) and
> **C = Contributor** (provides input), with no "R". If you use that one, write its legend into the document — never leave it ambiguous.

## Verification of the matrix (do this before publishing)

- [ ] **Each activity has exactly one "A"** — more than one means nobody decides
- [ ] No activity is left **without an "R"** — without an executor, it does not happen
- [ ] No person has accumulated "A" on activities they carry out themselves (conflict)
- [ ] Nobody appears as both "C" and "I" on the same activity (choose one)
- [ ] The matrix has been **read aloud with the team** — a matrix only works if it is agreed
- [ ] Client and supplier appear where they really have a role (not just out of courtesy)

## What it is for (the problem it solves)

- Aligning expectations before the problem appears
- Avoiding **overlapping responsibility** (two owners = no owner)
- Ensuring **every activity has a named owner**
- Settling on the spot the classic *"I thought it was you"*

## Signs of a badly made matrix
- An entire row of "I" (the person has no real role)
- The same person with "A" on everything (the bottleneck of decisions)
- No "C" at all (nobody is consulted — decision made in the dark)
- Client missing from the matrix (the client always has some role)
