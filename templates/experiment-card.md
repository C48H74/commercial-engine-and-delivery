# Experiment card

> **Rule:** one card = **one** variable. If you change two things, you will not know which one worked. The **stop rule is mandatory** — without it, the experiment becomes a habit.

---

```
DIAGNOSIS LINK:       [1 reach | 2 quality | 3 conversion | 4 capacity | 5 retention]

HYPOTHESIS:
  If I [SINGLE, CONCRETE CHANGE],
  then [METRIC] changes from [CURRENT VALUE] to [TARGET VALUE],
  because [REASON BASED ON EVIDENCE].

ONE VARIABLE:
  The only thing that changes:
  What stays the same (so as not to contaminate):

MEASUREMENT:
  What is counted:            [exact metric]
  Who counts it:              [name]
  Where it is recorded:       [system/spreadsheet]
  Reading frequency:          [daily | weekly]

TIMEFRAME:
  Start:                      [date]
  End:                        [date]
  Checkpoint:                 [intermediate date]

STOP RULE (mandatory):
  I stop and do not push if:  [objective criterion]

COST:
  Time:                       [hours]
  Money:                      [amount or R$ 0]

EVIDENCE NEEDED (degrades without it):
  ☐ client number   ☐ authorised proof   ☐ verifiable own data

RESULT (fill in at the end):
  Date:                       [date]
  Measured value:             [value]
  Reading:                    ☐ worked  ☐ did not work  ☐ inconclusive
  Decision:                   ☐ keep  ☐ revert  ☐ scale  ☐ new test
```

## How to write a good hypothesis

| Bad | Good |
|---|---|
| "I'll improve the email" | "if I change the subject to the number they mentioned, the reply rate goes from 4% to 8%" |
| "I'll prospect more" | "if I send 30 touches per week to segment X, I'll get 6 replies in 3 weeks" |
| "I'll improve the price" | "if I present the 3 tiers with the recommended one marked, the close rate goes from 15% to 25%" |

## Mistakes that invalidate the experiment
- changing two variables at the same time
- not defining the **current** value before starting (without a baseline, there is no reading)
- a deadline so long that the context changes in the middle
- no stop rule — the test becomes a permanent routine without a conclusion
- reading the result with confirmation bias (only seeing what confirms the hypothesis)
